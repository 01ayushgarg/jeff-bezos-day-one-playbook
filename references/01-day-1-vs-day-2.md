# 01 · Day 1 vs Day 2

> "Day 2 is stasis. Followed by irrelevance. Followed by excruciating, painful decline. Followed by death. And
> that is why it is always Day 1." [L2016]

## What he said, and how the idea changed

**1997: Day 1 meant the market was early.** In the first letter, *Day 1* was about timing: "But this is Day 1
for the Internet and, if we execute well, for Amazon.com." [L1997] The 1998 letter said the same, adding that
the opportunities ahead were bigger than those behind [L1998]. In 2001, after the crash, he told an
interviewer that the Internet, and Amazon in particular, "is still Chapter One." [AOA01]

**2009 to 2019: a sign-off.** From the 2009 letter on, almost every letter ends by attaching the 1997 letter and
saying the approach hasn't changed: "Our approach remains the same, and it's still Day 1." [L2009] Even in the
COVID letter: "Even in these circumstances, it remains Day 1." [L2019]

**2016: Day 1 became a culture to defend.** The 2016 letter answers a question from an all-hands meeting about
what Day 2 looks like. His answer is the line at the top of this chapter. The decline, he adds, would happen
in extreme slow motion; a big company can harvest Day 2 for decades before the end comes (our paraphrase
[L2016]). He offers a "starter pack" of four defenses (our summary of [L2016]):

| Defense | The Day 2 version | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| True customer obsession | Centering on competitors, products, technology or the business model | 02 |
| A skeptical view of proxies | Managing to a process, a survey or a metric instead of the outcome | 08 |
| Eager adoption of external trends | Fighting a powerful trend instead of using it | this chapter |
| High-velocity decision making | High-quality decisions, made slowly | 05 |

On trends: "If you fight them, you're probably fighting the future." [L2016] And on the whole list: "But we
have to choose it." [L2016]

**2015: size is the threat.** "We want to be a large company that's also an invention machine." [L2015] Scale
lets Amazon build things no start-up could, but "if we're not vigilant and thoughtful, size could slow us down
and diminish our inventiveness." [L2015] In 2018 he told the Economic Club the same thing about Amazon: "I want
it to have the heart and spirit of a small one." [ECW18]

**2023: Day 1 as renewal.** Five years later he described it as "a very simple, and I think age-old idea about
renewal and rebirth and every day is day one." [LEX405 01:21:56] One sentence there goes further than the
letters: "Self-consistency even can be a trap." [LEX405 01:21:56] His example is the list of tenets Amazon
writes for a program. Each list carries the words "Unless you know a better way" in parentheses, because "you
never want to get trapped by dogma." [LEX405 01:22:49] (The transcript spells *tenets* as *tenants* [sic].)

**Our reading of the evolution:** in 1997 Day 1 described the market. By 2016 it described the company's
behavior, and the threat had moved from competitors to Amazon's own size. By 2023 it is a habit of mind. The
four defenses are the most usable version, because each can be checked.

## How to apply it: the Day 2 audit (our suggestion)

Run this once a quarter, or whenever someone says *we used to be faster*. Each step tests one of his four
defenses; the thresholds are ours.

1. **Customer obsession.** List the last ten things you shipped. For each, write where the idea came from: a
   customer (complaint, request, observed behavior), a competitor, or internal preference. *Flag:* fewer than
   half started with a customer.
2. **Proxies.** List the five metrics in your weekly review. For each, write what it is a proxy for and who
   chose it. *Flag:* nobody in the room knows why one exists (chapter 08).
3. **Process as proxy.** Find the last bad outcome defended with *we followed the process.* Ask whether you own
   the process or it owns you (paraphrasing [L2016]).
4. **External trends.** Name the two or three biggest trends in your field. For each: are you using it, ignoring
   it, or fighting it? *Flag:* fighting.
5. **Decision velocity.** Take the last ten decisions and measure days from proposal to decision. Mark each one
   as a one-way or two-way door (chapter 05). *Flag:* two-way doors taking more than a week.
6. **Tenets.** For your biggest program, write five tenets and add *unless you know a better way* to the list
   [LEX405 01:22:49]. Ask the team which tenet they'd now drop.
7. **Score and act.** Mark each defense green, amber or red. Pick one red to fix this quarter, with an owner.

`templates/01-day-2-audit.md` has the form.

## A worked scenario (fictional)

A 60-person software company runs the audit. Of the last ten launches, three came from customer requests and
seven from a competitor's release notes. The weekly review tracks *tickets closed per agent*, which was chosen
four years ago to measure support quality; nobody now checks whether customers' problems are actually solved.
Pricing changes (a two-way door) take a median of 19 days because they go to the full leadership meeting. The
team has been resisting AI-assisted support because it *isn't accurate yet*.

Scores: customer obsession red, proxies red, trends amber, velocity red. They pick velocity first: two-way
doors under a set size now go to one named owner with a 48-hour deadline. Next quarter, proxies.

## Failure modes and limits (our reading)

- **Treating Day 1 as a slogan.** Writing *it's still Day 1* on the wall changes nothing. The four defenses are
  the content.
- **Confusing Day 1 with chaos.** The same letters insist on operational excellence and high standards
  (chapter 06). Day 1 is about speed and invention, not about skipping the work.
- **Throwing out what works.** He says you shouldn't discard history: there is real value in what has worked
  before, just don't follow it blindly (our paraphrase [LEX405 01:22:49]).
- **Small-company reading.** In a ten-person start-up, Day 2 is less likely to come from size and more likely
  from the founder's own habits. Run step 3 and step 5 on the founder.
- **Limits:** the four defenses are, in his word, a starter pack. He says he doesn't know the whole answer
  (our paraphrase [L2016]).

**Checks to run:**

3. Which trend are you fighting?
4. Which reversible decision has been waiting longest, and who is it waiting for?
