# 00 · Who Jeff Bezos is, for this playbook

This playbook is about one thing: how Jeff Bezos says he runs a company and makes decisions. It is built from
his 24 letters to Amazon shareholders (1997 to 2020) and three long interviews. It doesn't cover his private
life, his politics, his philanthropy or disputes about Amazon. Citation IDs are in `SOURCES.md`.

## How to weigh each source

- **The letters [L1997 to L2020]** are his most careful words: written, edited, and published by Amazon in
  its SEC filings. They are the backbone. They are also written to shareholders, so read them as advocacy for
  Amazon's approach, not as neutral analysis.
- **Lex Fridman #405 [LEX405 hh:mm:ss]** (December 2023) is the longest spoken source and the most recent.
  It's a human-made transcript with timestamps; the publisher notes it may contain errors. Only his lines are
  quoted.
- **Academy of Achievement [AOA01]** (May 2001) is the earliest interview, recorded right after the dot-com
  crash. It's the source for the regret minimization framework. Only his answers are quoted, not the
  Academy's biography or captions.
- **Economic Club of Washington [ECW18]** (September 2018), an interview by David Rubenstein. The Club
  publishes it as an *edited transcript*. Only his answers are quoted; the transcript's explanatory
  footnotes are the Club's, not his.

**Our reading** of the balance: for any idea that appears in a letter, the letter wins. Interviews add how he
explains the idea out loud, and what changed later.

## Read him with his own caveats

His 1997 letter says of its philosophy: "We aren't so bold as to claim that the above is the 'right'
investment philosophy, but it's ours" [L1997]. He repeated the point in 2015 about culture: "We never claim
that our approach is the right one – just that it's ours" [L2015].

On luck, in 2001: "any time a start-up company turns into a substantial company over the years, there was a
lot of luck involved." [AOA01] In 2015: "Luck plays an outsized role in every endeavor" [L2015].

**Our reading:** what follows is a set of procedures that a very large, very successful company says it uses.
Survivorship bias applies. The procedures here are ours, drawn from his words and labelled; judge them on
whether they help, not on his authority.

## What to borrow and what not to

- **Borrow:** the tests (one-way or two-way door, what won't change in ten years, is this metric a proxy),
  the writing discipline, and the habit of starting from the customer.
- **Translate with care:** many examples are about retail at enormous scale (fulfillment centers, price
  elasticity, AWS). A ten-person startup should apply the test, not copy the example.
- **Don't borrow:** the scale of the bets. *Multibillion-dollar failures* are, in his words, right for "a
  company of our size" [L2018]. Size your failures to your company.
