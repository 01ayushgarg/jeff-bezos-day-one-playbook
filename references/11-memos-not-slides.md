# 11 · Memos, not slides, and two kinds of meeting

> "I like a crisp document and a messy meeting." [LEX405 01:59:36]

## What he said

**No slides (2017).** "We don't do PowerPoint (or any other slide-oriented) presentations at Amazon. Instead, we
write narratively structured six-page memos." [L2017] Each meeting starts with everyone reading one in silence,
like a study hall (our paraphrase [L2017]). Quality varies; the best "have the clarity of angels singing."
[L2017]

**Great memos take time (2017).** A weak memo is usually a scope problem, not a skill problem: the writer
thought a day or two was enough (our paraphrase [L2017]; chapter 06). "The great memos are written and
re-written, shared with colleagues who are asked to improve the work, set aside for a couple of days, and then
edited again with a fresh mind." [L2017] By tradition, "authors' names never appear on the memos – the memo is
from the whole team." [L2017]

**Why memos beat slides (2023).** Six years later he gave the reasons in more detail:
- **Reading in the room.** People don't have time to read in advance, so they come having skimmed and bluff; the
  meeting starts with 30 minutes of silent reading (our paraphrase [LEX405 02:00:51, 02:01:17]).
- **Slides sell.** "PowerPoint is really designed to persuade." [LEX405 02:01:47] Internally, you're trying to
  find the truth, not to sell (our paraphrase [LEX405 02:01:47]).
- **Who does the work.** Slides are "easy for the author and hard for the audience. And a memo is the
  opposite." [LEX405 02:01:47]
- **Questions answer themselves.** He writes questions in the margins and crosses most of them out by the end,
  because the memo answers them (our paraphrase [LEX405 02:02:45]).
- **Sloppy thinking shows.** "you can hide a lot of sloppy thinking behind bullet points." [LEX405 02:04:20] A
  real memo has paragraphs with topic sentences, verbs and nouns (our paraphrase [LEX405 02:04:20]).
- **Authors go first.** The author has to be "very vulnerable" [LEX405 02:03:11], putting their thinking down
  before the senior people react, which fits his habit of speaking last (chapter 08).

**How long a memo takes: the estimate grew.** In 2017 a great memo "probably should take a week or more"
[L2017]. In 2023: "A good six-page memo might take two weeks to write." [LEX405 02:01:47] Either way, not a day.

**Two kinds of meeting (2023).** A problem-solving meeting starts with the crisp document and is allowed to
wander; it may run long, and that's the point (our paraphrase [LEX405 01:59:36]). A business review, weekly or
monthly, looks at the same metrics each time for incremental improvement: "Those meetings can be very
efficient. They can start on time and end on time." [LEX405 02:05:07]

**Writing as thinking, from the start (2001).** His first step in 1994 was a 30-page business plan, drafted on
the drive west. "The reality will never be the plan, but the discipline of writing the plan forces you to think
through some of the issues" [AOA01].

## How to apply it: the six-page memo (our suggestion)

1. **Decide the meeting type.** Problem to solve: write a memo. Regular review of known metrics: use a fixed
   dashboard and keep to time [LEX405 02:05:07].
2. **Set the scope before writing.** Give the author a week or more for an important memo, and say so
   [L2017].
3. **Write in full sentences.** Paragraphs with topic sentences, no bullet-point arguments [LEX405 02:04:20].
   Up to six pages; an appendix for data.
4. **Suggested structure (ours, not his):** the customer and the problem; what we know (data and anecdotes,
   chapter 08); the options; the recommendation and the door type (chapter 05); what would change our mind.
5. **Draft, share, rest, rewrite.** Ask colleagues to improve it, set it aside for two days, edit again
   [L2017].
6. **Read in the room.** Twenty to thirty minutes of silent reading; margin notes; no presentation
   [LEX405 02:00:51].
7. **Discuss, junior first.** Senior people speak last (chapter 08). Let the discussion wander if the problem
   is hard [LEX405 01:59:36].
8. **No author names.** The memo is the team's [L2017].

## A worked scenario (fictional)

A 90-person logistics start-up replaces its 40-slide monthly strategy deck with a six-page memo on one
question: should it enter cold-chain delivery? The first draft, written in an afternoon, is two pages of
bullets. Told to take a week, the author rewrites it as prose; writing the options section out in full shows
the team never priced refrigerated vans. The meeting opens with 25 minutes of silent reading. The most junior
analyst, speaking first, asks the question that changes the decision: who owns the spoiled goods? They
decide not to enter this year; the recurring ops review stays a 30-minute dashboard meeting.

## Failure modes and limits (our reading)

- **Slides pasted into a document.** Bullet lists in a memo template are still bullets.
- **Pre-reading instead of reading in the room.** He says people won't do it and will bluff [LEX405 02:01:17].
- **Every meeting a memo meeting.** Recurring reviews of known metrics should be short [LEX405 02:05:07].
- **Using length as a proxy for quality.** Six pages is a ceiling, not a target.
- **Limits:** the 2017 and 2023 accounts differ on how long a memo takes (a week or more, versus up to two
  weeks). The memo structure in step 4 is ours.
