# 13 · Differentiation, and creating more than you consume

> "If you want to be successful in business (in life, actually), you have to create more than you consume."
> [L2020]

## What he said

**Create more than you consume (2020).** In his last letter as CEO: "Your goal should be to create value for
everyone you interact with." [L2020] A business that doesn't, even if it looks successful, is on the way out
(our paraphrase [L2020]). He then estimated the value Amazon created in 2020 for each group, using deliberately
rough methods (his figures, our summary [L2020]):

| Group | His estimate for 2020 | How he estimated it |
|---|---|---|
| Shareholders | $21B | Net income |
| Employees | $91B | Pay plus benefits and payroll taxes |
| Third-party sellers | $25B | Low end of an internal estimate of seller profits |
| Customers | $164B | Time saved for Prime members, plus AWS customers' cost savings, doubled for speed |
| **Total** | **$301B** | |

He was open that the AWS figure had high error bars (our paraphrase [L2020]). His point: "Draw the box big
around all of society, and you'll find that invention is the root of all real value creation." [L2020]

**Differentiation as a test for new businesses (2006).** Before starting a new business, Amazon wanted good
returns on capital, the potential to be large, an underserved market, and the ability to bring real
differentiation for customers (our summary [L2006]). Physical stores failed all but one test. Among the
reasons: "we don't have any ideas for how to build a physical world store experience that's meaningfully
differentiated for customers." [L2006]

**The test, twelve years later (2018).** Asked for 20 years about physical stores, he said he always answered yes,
but "only when we have a differentiated offering, something that's not 'me, too'" [ECW18]. When Amazon tries
a *me too* service, "we tend to get beaten." [ECW18] The store Amazon eventually built started from one goal:
"Get rid of the worst thing about physical retail: checkout lines." [L2018] **Our reading:** the 2006 answer
was no, not never. The test stayed the same; the idea arrived later.

**Don't copy the thing you're replacing (2007).** Designing the Kindle, the team decided "we could never
out-book the book." [L2007] It had to add things a printed book can't do, like looking up words or getting
a book in 60 seconds, the same way the early online store didn't try to copy book signings (our paraphrase
[L2007]).

**The universe wants you to be typical (2020).** His closing lesson as CEO. Drawing on a passage from Richard
Dawkins about living things working constantly to stay different from their surroundings, he asks: "In what
ways does the world pull at you in an attempt to make you normal?" [L2020] And: "The world wants you to be
typical – in a thousand ways, it pulls at you. Don't let it happen." [L2020] Distinctiveness has a cost: "You
have to pay a price for your distinctiveness, and it's worth it." [L2020] His sign-off: "be kind, be original,
create more than you consume, and never, never, never let the universe smooth you into your surroundings."
[L2020]

**Stubborn on vision (2015, 2020).** In 2015, on the failures before Marketplace, Amazon "stayed stubborn on the
vision" [L2015]. In 2020: "On the details, we at Amazon are always flexible, but on matters of vision we are
stubborn and relentless." [L2020] A shorter version, *be stubborn on vision and flexible on details*, is often
attributed to him; in these sources it appears only in the interviewer's closing words of the 2023 podcast, not
as Bezos speaking, so it isn't quoted as his.

## How to apply it: the value ledger and the distinctiveness check (our suggestion)

**The value ledger** (once a year):

1. **List everyone you touch:** customers, employees, suppliers or partners, owners, and anyone else your work
   affects.
2. **Estimate the value created for each,** in money where you can, with the method next to the number. Be
   conservative and show your error bars, as he did [L2020].
3. **Compare with what you take.** Revenue from customers, effort from employees, margin from partners.
4. **Flag any group where you consume more than you create.** That's the business risk, not just an ethical
   one [L2020].

**The distinctiveness check** (once a year, or before a new product):

1. **Write what makes you different,** in one line a customer would recognize.
2. **List the pulls toward typical:** what competitors, investors, hires or customers keep asking you to copy
   [L2020].
3. **For any new product,** run the differentiation test: underserved need, a real customer-facing difference,
   size, returns. If it's *me too*, wait [L2006, ECW18].
4. **Don't out-book the book.** If you're replacing something, list what the old thing does best and refuse to
   compete on that; list what only you can do [L2007].
5. **Name the vision and the details.** Write the one thing you won't change, and three details you're willing
   to change this year [L2020].
6. **Budget the energy.** Distinctiveness costs effort. Name who maintains it [L2020].

## A worked scenario (fictional)

A regional bookshop chain of six stores runs the ledger: customers get advice and events (they estimate the value
of curated picks from surveys, with wide error bars); staff pay is below the local median; publishers get
strong sales of debut novels. The flag is staff: they consume more effort than they reward. The distinctiveness
check names the one-line difference as *booksellers who read everything they recommend*. The pulls toward
typical: investors want a café and gifts. A planned online store fails the differentiation test (it would be
*me too*), so they drop it and build a staff-written newsletter instead, and raise pay to keep the readers
who write it.

## Failure modes and limits (our reading)

- **Inflated ledgers.** If every number is generous, the exercise is marketing. Use his conservative approach.
- **Different for its own sake.** The 2006 test asks for differentiation customers care about, not novelty.
- **Stubborn on details.** The reverse of his rule: holding on to a feature while the vision drifts.
- **Limits:** the value figures are his 2020 estimates, not audited. The 2020 letter also discusses employee
  relations and a union vote; those passages are outside this playbook's scope.

**Use it now:** the value ledger above; the differentiation test in `templates/02-working-backwards-brief.md`.

**Checks to run:**
1. Which group do you take more from than you give?
2. What is pulling you toward normal right now?
3. Is your next product *me too*?
4. What are you stubborn about, and is it the vision or a detail?
